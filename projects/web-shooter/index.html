Web-Shooter

A wrist-mounted, spring-launched tether with motorized auto-retract, built on an ESP32. Designed, tested, and measured like a real product.

I wanted to build something I'd actually want to wear: my own take on Spider-Man's web-shooter. Underneath the fun, it's a compact mechatronic system with a spring launch, a one-way clutch spool, closed-loop motor control, and sensor-based auto-stop, all in a package light enough to wear on a wrist.

My goal is to practice the full engineering loop: set specs, predict performance with hand calculations and FEA, build, measure, and improve the design using statistics.

How it works

  1. Launch: a compression spring pushes a 3D-printed dart with a magnet tip. A servo releases a sear latch, so the servo only moves a catch and never carries the full spring load.
  2. Payout: the tether unwinds from a spool that runs on a one-way clutch bearing, so the spool free-wheels while the line pays out, with no clutch to actuate.
  3. Retract: the same clutch locks in the other direction, and an encoder gear motor reels the line in under PID speed control.
  4. Auto-stop: a current sensor detects the load spike when the dart docks and stops the motor.

Key hand calculations (planned)

Spring. Dart mass ≈ 15 g at 5 m/s → KE = ½mv² ≈ 0.19 J. Assuming ~50% spring-to-dart efficiency, the spring must store ≈ 0.38 J. With 40 mm compression, k = 2E/x² ≈ 470 N/m, and the peak latch force is kx ≈ 19 N.

Gear ratio. Retracting 2 m in under 5 s needs v ≥ 0.4 m/s. With a 40 mm spool, n = 60v/(πd) ≈ 190 rpm minimum, so a ~50:1 N20 gear motor rated above 200 rpm fits.

Resolution. 14 encoder counts per motor revolution × 50:1 = 700 counts per spool revolution, which works out to ≈ 0.18 mm of line per count.

Test plan

  • Launch efficiency: measured speed vs. stored spring energy, at three spring compressions.
  • Repeatability: 30 shots, with mean, standard deviation, and Cpk against the 5.0 ± 0.5 m/s spec.
  • FEA: stress in the latch and wrist mount under the 19 N spring load, with a target safety factor ≥ 2.
  • Controls: retract speed step response before and after PID tuning.
  • Design for assembly: count the parts and time the assembly, then cut both in a redesign using keyed (poka-yoke) parts that only fit one way.

Safety

The launch energy is kept below 0.5 J, the dart has a soft tip, and it's always tethered. Testing is done with eye protection and the launcher is never aimed at people.
